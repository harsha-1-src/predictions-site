Methodology

Two independent hobby models, one per sport. Both are gradient-boosted tree models (XGBoost) evaluated walk-forward only: every prediction is made using data available strictly before the game, and the models never see future data during evaluation. Published picks are logged before kickoff/tip-off and graded after the fact, unedited.

The honest betting finding

Neither model beats the betting market. Against closing spreads both sit near 50% against the spread, which after the bookmaker’s vig means a negative return on investment — no betting edge.

  • NFL backtest: 49.8% ATS, -4.1% ROI
  • NBA backtest: 50.2% ATS, -3.7% ROI

That is the expected, honest result against closing lines, which are extremely efficient. These models are for fun and for tracking calibrated win probabilities, not for beating the market.

NFL model

A market-anchored XGBoost model built on nflverse data. Features include exponentially weighted (EWMA) team form, an Elo rating, and a closing-line prior that anchors the model to the market’s opening information. The totals head additionally uses weather, and a rolling starting-QB CPOE feature tracks quarterback play.

NBA model

A stats-only XGBoost model anchored on an Elo rating — no betting market inputs at all. Features include exponentially weighted four-factors (shooting, turnovers, rebounding, free throws) and rest/schedule effects. Because it uses no market data, its picks are made without ever seeing a line — the odds below are attached afterwards, purely to settle the published units.

Where the odds come from

NFL spreads come from nflverse historical closing lines. Those are a line only — nflverse does not publish the price that went with it — so NFL against-the-spread results are settled at the standard -110 convention. That figure is a stated assumption, not a recorded price, and it is the only place on this site where a price is assumed at all.

NBA moneylines and spreads come from live odds APIs read at prediction time: ParlayAPI first, with The Odds API as the fallback (both on their free tiers). Those carry a real price, so NBA bets settle at the exact price logged with the pick — often -110 on a spread, but never assumed to be.

Every price is recorded alongside the pick before the game and is never back-filled or invented afterwards. Coverage is per game, not per league: some games get a spread and a moneyline, some only one of the two, some neither. A game played with no odds on record is shown as ungraded for units — an em dash in the units column, and counted in the note under the P/L table — rather than being assumed into a price it never had. Days with no odds at all simply contribute no units.

Prediction horizon

Live picks are published only for games starting 3–7 days out. Closer than that, a prediction is still moving: injuries, rest decisions and line moves keep changing the inputs, and republishing a pick every few hours would make the log a diary of revisions rather than a record of forecasts. Further out than 7 days, no prediction is made at all — the fixture is on the schedule, but who will actually be available to play is not yet knowable.

A few picks on this site were logged long before that policy existed, at much longer lead times. The prediction log is append-only, so they are kept and labelled rather than deleted: you will find them on the track record badged outside window, with no units. They are excluded from every P/L figure — crediting units to picks the current policy would never have published would overstate what this site actually does — but they remain counted in the straight-up accuracy record, because they were genuine graded predictions and dropping them would flatter the model. Wherever those two scopes differ, the page says so.

Backtests are unaffected. This is a live-publication policy, not a modelling change: the walk-forward evaluation below scores every historical game in its seasons, at whatever lead time the backtest implies.

Evaluation

Backtests are strictly walk-forward: the model is retrained through season N and scored on season N + 1, then the window rolls forward. Accuracy above is straight-up (picking the winner); the Brier score measures probability calibration (lower is better); margin MAE is the mean absolute error of the predicted point margin.

These are the outputs of a hobby statistical model, published for fun and transparency. Not betting advice.
